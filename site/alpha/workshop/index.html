A life skills workshop

Cut and Donate 13" of James' Hair

I've spent almost two years growing my hair out to 13" long, but I want to cut it. As I was learning about Alpha School, your life skills workshops, and reflecting on the time I've spent working with kids I was excited to learn about the science behind many beliefs I had and the practices that come from them.

Treating kids like they're competent, having high standards and high support, believing that they are limitless, the list is limitless. I know that the biggest smiles, the biggest transformations, I've worked with kids to get, have been from my trust in them, and my walking alongside them.

So consider this my olive branch, my vote of confidence, my commitment to this belief.

Moreover, I believe a workshop

Having an adult trust them to cut off 2 years of hair is hugely valuable for their confidence.

But it also hits the following values of yours:

  • Relationship Building & empathy: the hair goes to a kid who lost theirs; they're not doing a craft, they're changing a stranger's life. And they see it modeled right there in the chair: a barber's whole business runs on strangers trusting them enough to put sharp tools to their head and face. Relationships are just trust, made physical.
  • Generosity, “spend and donate”: the same muscle as Alpha Savers and the Water for Life Project, except this time they give something that came off a person who trusted them.

What it looks like

  1. Kids work on a BrainLift (or a lesson) about how many kids their age lose their hair to chemo, alopecia, and other causes, and what that's like.
  2. Talk with a stylist about their business: the lessons from years of people in the chair, and how they build real relationships.
  3. Learn the basic parts of a hair donation.
  4. Make the measurements, the cuts, then package and mail the hair.
  5. Learn the donating side: how nonprofits work and where the hair goes.
  6. Wigs for Kids: meet the org the donation goes to.

Logistics

  • Donation org: Wigs for Kids (12" minimum). My 13" clears it.
  • Safety: a licensed stylist performs and oversees the actual cut; kids do the ceremonial cuts, measuring, and packaging.
  • Materials: shears, hair ties, measuring tape, sealable bag + padded mailer, donation form, thank-you template.
