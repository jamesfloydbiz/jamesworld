1 · Who we call first

The ideal partner profile

An ideal partner "has a business with a warm audience full of people just like your customers" (Hormozi). For the pilot, score each lead 0–2 on these five and call the highest first.

  1. ESA families already come in. Says "ESA approved" or "ClassWallet vendor," runs homeschool classes, or shows up in Pathfinder's vendor spend sheet.
  2. Instruction, not open play. Classes, lessons, camps or leagues with a coach. These fit ESA rules best and keep guarantee claims low.
  3. Empty weekday daytime slots. Homeschool classes fill hours that sit empty between school-day mornings and after-school rush.
  4. Cash flow hurts. They limit how ESA families can pay (semester only, no drop-ins), or complain about reimbursement waits.
  5. One decision-maker. An owner or local franchisee who can say yes this week. Big chains benefit less (Kate call).

2 · The offer

One sentence, then the kit

Make more from the ESA families you serve, and meet the ones you don't yet. Get paid at the point of sale instead of waiting weeks on reimbursements, from families who already have ESA money to spend, without new paperwork, software or support, so you can start this week. Sound good?Adapted from the Partnerships Plan offer, built on the value equation: dream outcome (cash now, fuller classes), likelihood (families already have ESA money), time (paid at swipe, live this week), effort (no ClassWallet setup, kit done for them).

What raises the value

  • Paid at the point of sale. Visa on their existing reader.
  • Access to ESA parents. Partner listing, a feature in Pathfinder's family emails, the events calendar.
  • A co-hosted Family Day at their gym (Gold Medal model).
  • White-labeled lead magnets. Their own "Is it ESA-allowable?" QR check and a homeschool PE guide with their logo.
  • Status. Pilot partner, then Champion badge.

What we never promise

  • That every class is ESA-allowable. Open gym and drop-ins are often denied as recreation.
  • That the guarantee covers the gym. It covers families for pre-approved purchases, up to the limits.
  • A number of new families. We promise the listing, emails and family day, not a headcount.
  • That there is no cost. They pay their usual card processing fee.
3 · The script

Five steps, every line ends in a question mark

Same frame as TPC. Pick the channel. The words change; the five steps don't.

  1. 1Intro

    "Hi, I'm James with Pathfinder. Are you the owner? Quick question—"

    Warm and short. Ask for the decision-maker up front so you don't pitch the front desk.

  2. 2Qualifying question

    "Do any of your families pay with ESA money?"

    Then wait. A yes means you go on. A "not many" is a door: "How full are your weekday mornings?"

  3. 3Short story

    "The reason I ask: a lot of ESA parents tell us they skip classes because they have to pay out of pocket and wait weeks for the state to pay them back. Does that sound familiar?"

    Swap in a real story from a parent or owner as soon as you have one. Never make one up.

  4. 4Presentation and close

    "Pathfinder is a Visa card for ESA families, so they pay you at the front desk like any card and you're paid right then. We send ESA families your way and pay you for every family who gets the card through you—what's better for you, getting set up this week or next?"

    One breath, no pause before the close. The close is a choice between two yeses, like "cash or Venmo?"

  5. 5Rehash

    "While you do the sign-up—how long have you been running homeschool classes?" … "By the way, do you know any other gym owners with ESA families?"

    Chat while they sign up (create a personal relationship), so the yes holds. Then ask for the referral and go.

  1. 1Call out (to parents and partners you already know)

    "Quick question—do you know anyone who runs a gym, a dojo or a swim school?"

    Hormozi's call-out: ask your own customers who owns the business. Pathfinder cardholders already shop there.

  2. 2Qualifying question

    "Do they have homeschool or ESA families?"

  3. 3Short story

    "The reason I ask: we're picking 10 Phoenix gyms to get paid at the front desk by ESA families instead of waiting on reimbursements. Would they want that?"

  4. 4Presentation and close

    "Could you text them an intro now, or should I mention your name when I call?"

  5. 5Rehash

    "Thank you. Anyone else who'd want to hear about it?"

4 · Objections

Never argue and never run longer than the objection. Every reply ends in a question.

5 · Follow-up

Two weeks, five touches

  1. Day 1. Walk in or call during a quiet weekday hour. Run the five steps. Leave the pilot page link.
  2. Day 2. Send the first email with the ?for= link made for them.
  3. Day 4. Text or DM: "Did the overview make sense? Want me to walk you through the certification?"
  4. Day 7. Drop by with the printed homeschool PE guide, their logo on it. Ask for the close again.
  5. Day 14. Last touch, the takeaway: "Sounds like now isn't the time. Should I close your spot in the pilot?"

Track every conversation

Log how each one ended with the TPC tracker marks, the second it ends. Lots of talks, few yeses: fix the pitch. Few talks: fix the pace. Both fine and still dead: fix the attitude.

After the yes: activation

  1. They finish the 13-minute certification.
  2. We send the kit: QR check, PE guide with their logo, front-desk card.
  3. We book their Family Day date.
  4. First 3 approved families moves them up a tier.
6 · Payouts

Three tiers, capped by what a family is worth

Hormozi's rule: pay for the exact action you want, and keep lifetime gross profit to acquisition cost at 3:1 or better. Use three tiers, not two or five.

TierEarned byPayout per approved family
PartnerFinishing the certification$15 (25% of max)
Pilot partnerKit up and first 3 families approved$30 (about 50–65% of max)
ChampionKeeping that pace 3 months running$45 (100% of max)
To confirm with Sid and Amaan: the plan doc notes "$45 per app" and "$15 per card app" as what's done now. I used $45 as the maximum. That maximum should equal one third of a cardholder's lifetime gross profit; if it's lower, scale all three tiers down.
7 · Launch

Whisper, tease, shout

Whisper · 6+ weeks out

Partners hint to their families that something is coming for ESA parents. Keep it short and spaced out.

Tease · 6 weeks to 7 days

Reveal it: "ESA families can now pay at our front desk." Announce the Family Day date.

Shout · last 7 days

Reminders at 3 days, 2, 1, then hours. Add a bonus for families who sign up at the event.

Keep them promoting

Two partner emails a week: one with fresh content, one with standings. New content pack around week 3.
