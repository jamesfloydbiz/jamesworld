Write-up

What's new on the frontier

I wanted one picture of where education is moving. Who's building, what works, what's missing. So I mapped it.

The short version: AI tutors work when they're built like tutors and kids use them. Reading is going back to phonics. Tests are starting to change. Most of the boldest new schools have no independent evidence yet.

AI tutors

The research is catching up.

At Harvard, a structured GPT-4 tutor beat an active-learning physics class, in less time. In Nigeria, six weeks of after-school Microsoft Copilot raised English 0.23 standard deviations. In Ghana, Rori tutors math over WhatsApp on basic phones. One hour a week, 0.36 SD, about $5 a student.

Setup matters. In Turkey, plain ChatGPT raised practice grades 48%, then cut exam scores 17% once it was gone. Kids let it do the thinking. A hints-only version mostly avoided that. At one U.S. university, an AI tutor lowered final grades.

Use matters most. Khanmigo ran two years in Tennessee and gained about what plain Khan practice did. The median student messaged it on a third of practice days. Stanford's 2026 review agrees. Kids do better with the AI on, and the gain can fade once it's off.

So the tutor isn't the bottleneck. Getting a kid to do the work is. That's why the schools here care so much about the adults in the room.

Mastery and knowledge graphs

The bigger shift is software that picks what you learn next.

Math Academy maps math into a graph of topics, finds where you are, and teaches only what you're ready for. The system times your reviews. Squirrel AI does the same in China, at a very fine grain. ALEKS and MATHia have done versions for decades. In England, Eedi tags every wrong answer with the misconception behind it.

The idea has strong evidence. A big 2013 review rated only two study techniques high: practice testing and spaced practice. In India, Mindspark's adaptive software raised math 0.37 SD after school in Delhi and 0.22 SD in Rajasthan's government schools.

Evidence for single products is thinner. Math Academy has no independent study yet.

Reading

Back to basics, and it's working.

Mississippi passed a literacy law in 2013. In 2024 its 4th graders beat the national average in reading for the first time. England made every Year 1 child take a phonics check. The pass rate went from 58% to 82%.

Meanwhile the U.S. is sliding. On NAEP 2024, the weakest 8th grade readers scored their lowest since the test began in 1992. 12th grade reading hit a record low.

Amira, Ello and Google Read Along listen to a kid read aloud and correct each word, like a parent would. Pratham's Teaching at the Right Level needs no tech. A one-page test sorts kids by level instead of grade. Its 40-day camps in Uttar Pradesh raised scores 0.70 SD.

Assessment

Tests are changing for the first time in decades.

The Duolingo English Test writes its own questions with AI, so the bank is too big to memorize. Texas machine-scores written answers. Reading rescore requests went from 2,758 to 64,489 in two years. It's also swapping its one big test for three shorter ones through the year. PISA tested creative thinking in 64 school systems in 2022. Singapore led. The U.S. didn't take part. Georgia Tech and NYU run oral exams where an AI asks the questions.

New schools

The schools interest me most.

Alpha says its kids grow 2.6x faster on MAP tests, in two hours a day. Primer and Prenda let a teacher run a tiny microschool, often on state ESA money. Online schools like Sora and Prisma run on projects and coaches.

Almost none have independent evidence yet. Alpha's numbers are its own. That doesn't mean they fail. Someone should check.

Help for teachers

Some of the best results come from helping adults. Stanford's Tutor CoPilot whispers suggestions to human tutors mid-session. Students were 4 points likelier to master a topic, 9 with the weakest tutors. About $20 per tutor a year. In England, a proper trial found Oak's Aila cut lesson-planning time with no drop in quality.

The gaps

What nobody has built or proven yet. The good stuff.

A test for thinking that changes the problem

Nothing at scale tests reframing a problem. Ender attacking the giant. The matchstick moved to the minus sign. PISA's creativity test scores ideas, not breaking the frame. Insight puzzles die once a friend spoils the answer. AI could give every student a new one. Nobody does yet. I built a first version.

Spotting the unsolvable

"A captain has 26 sheep and 10 goats. How old is the captain?" Nothing at scale tests whether kids notice.

Gains that last

Most AI tutor studies test right away. Few check a month later, or without the AI. Stanford's 2026 review says gains can fade without it. The first company to show lasting gains wins.

Getting kids to use it

Khanmigo didn't fail on tech. Kids rarely messaged it. Motivation is the unsolved problem. Alpha says its guides and incentives fix it. Nobody has checked independently.

Evidence for the new schools

Alpha, Primer, Prenda, Sora, Synthesis, Math Academy. Most of my favorites have no independent study. An outside study of the 2-hour school day would be among the most important in education.

Process, not just answers

PISA 2012 logged every click as students solved problems, which shows how they think. Mostly hiring games use it. I found no K-12 product that does.

The U.S. on the world's creativity test

The U.S. skipped PISA's creative thinking test in 2022. We don't know where American 15-year-olds stand.

Africa and Latin America

Some of the biggest AI tutor effects so far come from Ghana, Nigeria and India. Large trials are rare. Colombia has trials registered, no results yet.

People

50 voices, and where they agree

I picked 50 people whose work shapes where learning is going. Researchers, builders and the sharpest critics. Then I tracked what each has said in public on seven big questions.

Most have said nothing on most questions, so the counts are small. A blank means no public stance. It doesn't mean neutral.

Where they agree

Learning takes effort. Tools that take the effort away take the learning with it. Bjork calls it desirable difficulty. Agarwal and Roediger call it retrieval practice. Willingham: "memory is the residue of thought."

Be careful with AI doing kids' thinking. 13 of the 18 who've taken a side are cautious or against. 4 of the 5 in favor build or sell AI tutors.

Novices need to be taught directly. 8 of 9 back explicit teaching. Jo Boaler is the holdout.

Phonics first, but phonics isn't enough. All 5 who've weighed in back phonics. Most add that knowledge, comprehension and writing matter once kids can decode.

Teach kids at their level, not their grade. 9 for, 1 cautious. From Rukmini Banerji's camps in India to Alpha and Math Academy.

Keep a human in the loop. Tutor CoPilot made human tutors better. Even Sal Khan now says the biggest lever is "investing in the human systems."

No phones in class. Nobody here argues for them.

Where they don't

Can AI teach the core subjects? MacKenzie Price and Joe Liemandt run Alpha on it. Khan, DiCerbo and Mollick are hopeful. Dan Meyer and Benjamin Riley say no. Most researchers sit in the middle.

Do phone bans raise scores? Jonathan Haidt is pushing bans state by state. A 2026 national study co-written by Dee and Duckworth found phone use dropped and scores barely moved.

Discovery or direct teaching in math. Boaler on one side. Sweller, Kirschner and Skycak on the other.

School choice. Michael Petrilli is for it, with accountability. Randi Weingarten and Diane Ravitch are against.

Does growth mindset work? Carol Dweck and David Yeager point to a big 2019 trial. Critics point to replications that found little.

The surprise

The biggest AI tutor optimist, Sal Khan, said in April 2026 that for a lot of students Khanmigo was a "non-event."
